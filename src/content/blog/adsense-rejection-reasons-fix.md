Getting rejected by Google AdSense is discouraging, especially when the rejection email offers little specific guidance. "We didn't approve your site" does not tell you whether the problem is content volume, missing legal pages, navigation, or something else entirely.

Most rejections among solo bloggers fall into a predictable set of categories — and most are fixable without starting over on a new domain. AdSense reviewers evaluate whether your site looks like a legitimate publication with original content, clear ownership, and a reasonable experience for visitors.

This guide walks through the most common AdSense rejection reasons in 2026 and concrete fixes for each. Work through the checklist before reapplying; repeated applications without meaningful changes waste time and can delay approval further.

## Before you reapply: understand the review process

AdSense reviews are semi-automated with human oversight. Reviewers (or systems trained on reviewer decisions) check:

- **Content quality and quantity** — enough original articles on real topics
- **Site completeness** — About, Contact, Privacy Policy, functional navigation
- **Policy compliance** — no prohibited content, copyright issues, or deceptive layouts
- **User experience** — readable text, working links, mobile-friendly layout
- **Site ownership** — verified domain, no parked or under-construction pages

Reapply only after visible improvements. Submitting the same site three times in a week without changes rarely helps.

## Rejection reason 1: Not enough content

**What Google means:** Your site has too few pages, articles are too short, or content looks placeholder-thin.

**Common symptoms:**

- Five or fewer published articles
- Posts under 500 words with little substance
- "Coming soon" or lorem ipsum pages indexed
- Category pages that are empty shells

**How to fix:**

1. Publish **15–20 solid articles** before reapplying (minimum practical threshold; more helps in competitive niches)
2. Aim for **800+ words** on most posts — tutorials and guides naturally hit this
3. Remove or noindex placeholder pages
4. Ensure each article solves a specific problem or answers a clear question

Quality beats quantity, but quantity proves commitment. A tech blog with twelve in-depth tutorials stands a better chance than three long posts and twenty thin product roundups scraped from manufacturer specs.

## Rejection reason 2: Content quality and originality

**What Google means:** Content appears duplicated, auto-generated, low-effort, or copied from other sources.

**Common symptoms:**

- Articles rewritten from competitors with no original insight
- AI-generated walls of text without editing or personal experience
- Aggregated news with no commentary
- Spelling and grammar errors throughout

**How to fix:**

1. Add **original perspective** — personal experience, screenshots, step-by-step tests you ran
2. Edit AI-assisted drafts heavily — fact-check, restructure, inject voice
3. Remove or rewrite any content that duplicates your other sites or syndicated sources
4. Use canonical tags if you cross-post legitimately elsewhere

AdSense does not ban AI content categorically, but unedited generic content fails human quality checks. Your About page should reflect a real person behind the site.

## Rejection reason 3: Missing or inadequate legal pages

**What Google means:** Required policy pages are missing, incomplete, or inaccessible.

**Required pages:**

| Page | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| Privacy Policy | Discloses data collection, cookies, third-party ads |
| About | Who runs the site, what it covers |
| Contact | Email or form for reaching you |

**How to fix:**

1. Publish a **Privacy Policy** that mentions Google AdSense, cookies, and analytics — use a generator as a starting point, then customize
2. Write an **About page** with your name, background, and site mission
3. Add a **Contact page** with a working email or form — test it yourself
4. Link all three in your header or footer on every page

Privacy Policy must be reachable from the homepage without hunting. Footer links are standard.

## Rejection reason 4: Navigation and site structure problems

**What Google means:** Reviewers cannot easily browse your site or understand what it offers.

**Common symptoms:**

- No menu or broken navigation
- Homepage is a single paragraph with no links to articles
- Infinite scroll with no pagination or categories
- Broken internal links everywhere

**How to fix:**

1. Add clear **header navigation**: Home, Blog, About, Contact
2. Show **recent or featured articles** on the homepage
3. Fix **404 errors** — run a crawl with Screaming Frog or check Search Console
4. Use **readable URLs** — `/blog/adsense-rejection-reasons-fix/` not `/p?id=3847`

Static site generators like Astro make clean structure easy. WordPress users should simplify bloated menus and remove unused default pages.

## Rejection reason 5: Prohibited or restricted content

**What Google means:** Site covers topics AdSense restricts or bans.

**Restricted categories include:**

- Adult or sexually explicit content
- Violence, hate speech, harassment
- Illegal drugs, weapons, counterfeit goods
- Copyright infringement or piracy
- Dangerous or deceptive content
- Excessive profanity in professional contexts

**How to fix:**

1. Read the [AdSense program policies](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/48182) in full
2. Remove or heavily edit non-compliant articles
3. If your niche is borderline (health claims, finance advice), add disclaimers and cite sources
4. Do not host user-generated content without moderation — comment spam can trigger policy flags

Tech tutorials, productivity, web development, and publishing advice are generally safe. Problems arise when sites drift into scraped downloads, keygens, or misleading "get rich quick" claims.

## Rejection reason 6: Invalid or insufficient traffic sources

**What Google means:** Traffic appears artificial, incentivized, or from low-quality sources.

**Common symptoms:**

- Buying traffic or using click exchanges
- Pop-ups that force accidental clicks
- Misleading social posts that bait clicks

**How to fix:**

1. Grow traffic organically through SEO and legitimate social sharing
2. Remove aggressive pop-ups before applying — add email capture after approval if needed
3. Do not click your own ads after approval (policy violation with permanent ban risk)

You do not need massive traffic to get approved. You need **real** traffic from normal sources — or enough indexed content that reviewers see publishing intent.

## Rejection reason 7: Site not ready or under construction

**What Google means:** Site looks unfinished, domain is parked, or main pages return errors.

**Common symptoms:**

- "Under construction" banners
- Default hosting placeholder pages still indexed
- SSL certificate errors or mixed content warnings
- Homepage returns 500 errors intermittently

**How to fix:**

1. Deploy a complete site on your **custom domain** with valid HTTPS
2. Remove hosting provider default pages from Google's index
3. Verify all pages load on mobile and desktop
4. Check Search Console for crawl errors and fix before reapplying

Apply from your production URL, not `localhost` or a staging subdomain with noindex accidentally removed.

## Rejection reason 8: Domain age and trust signals

**What Google means:** Very new domains with no history may face stricter scrutiny — though this is rarely stated explicitly.

**How to fix:**

1. Publish consistently for **4–8 weeks** before reapplying
2. Build **Search Console** verification and submit sitemap
3. Earn a few **natural backlinks** — guest comments with value, directory listings for developer blogs, sharing on relevant communities (not spam)
4. Keep **WHOIS privacy off** or ensure contact info is consistent — minor signal, but helps legitimacy

Domain age alone is not a blocker. Many new sites get approved with strong content. Weak content on an old domain still fails.

## Rejection reason 9: Poor mobile experience

**What Google means:** Site is hard to read or use on mobile devices.

**How to fix:**

1. Use a **responsive theme** — test on real phones, not just browser resize
2. Fix **font sizes** — body text at least 16px
3. Avoid horizontal scrolling and overlapping elements
4. Check **Core Web Vitals** in Search Console — poor LCP and CLS suggest layout problems

Static sites with minimal JavaScript often pass mobile checks easily. Heavy ad placeholders before approval are unnecessary — add ad slots after acceptance.

## Rejection reason 10: Previous policy violations

**What Google means:** The account or domain had prior AdSense bans or policy strikes.

**How to fix:**

- If you were banned previously, **do not create new accounts** — that violates policy permanently
- Contact AdSense support through the official channel if you believe a mistake occurred
- For a new site on a clean account, ensure no connection to previously banned properties (same thin content republished elsewhere)

This category is the hardest to fix. Prevention matters: read policies before applying.

## Step-by-step reapplication checklist

Work through this list in order:

- [ ] 15+ original articles, mostly 800+ words
- [ ] About, Contact, Privacy Policy linked in footer
- [ ] Privacy Policy mentions advertising and cookies
- [ ] Custom domain with valid HTTPS
- [ ] Working navigation and no broken links
- [ ] Mobile-friendly layout tested on devices
- [ ] No prohibited content
- [ ] Search Console verified, sitemap submitted
- [ ] Homepage showcases your content
- [ ] Removed placeholder and "coming soon" pages
- [ ] Waited at least 2–4 weeks since last rejection with real improvements

Then reapply through the AdSense dashboard. One thoughtful reapplication beats three hasty ones.

## After approval: do not undo your work

Approved publishers get banned for:

- Clicking their own ads
- Encouraging clicks ("support my site by clicking ads")
- Placing ads on policy-violating new content
- Sudden flood of low-quality pages

Maintain the quality bar that earned approval. Expand content thoughtfully, keep legal pages updated, and monitor site health in Search Console.

AdSense rejection is feedback, not a verdict on your blogging career. Most successful independent publishers were rejected at least once. Fix the structural issues, deepen your archive, and reapply when the site genuinely looks like a publication you'd trust as a reader — because that is exactly what the reviewer asks.
