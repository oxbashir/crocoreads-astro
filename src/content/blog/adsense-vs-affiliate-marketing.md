New publishers face the same question after a few months of writing: how does this site pay for itself? The two most accessible answers are **Google AdSense** (display ads paid per impression or click) and **affiliate marketing** (commissions when readers buy through your links). Both work on content sites. They reward different content types, traffic levels, and maintenance styles.

Choosing wrong leads to frustration — AdSense pennies on a finance blog built for affiliates, or affiliate links on a news commentary site with no commercial intent. This guide compares both models honestly so you can decide what fits your niche, audience, and stage of growth.

## How Google AdSense works

AdSense is an advertising network. Google matches ads to your pages based on content and visitor signals. You paste ad code (or enable Auto ads); Google handles sales, serving, and billing. You earn primarily through:

- **CPC (cost per click)** — Revenue when a visitor clicks an ad
- **CPM (cost per mille)** — Revenue per thousand impressions on some placements

Payment is passive relative to affiliates: no product picks to refresh weekly, no merchant relationship management. Revenue scales with **traffic volume** and **niche RPM** (revenue per thousand pageviews).

### AdSense pros

- Low operational overhead after approval
- Works on broad informational content — tutorials, explainers, news commentary
- Single payment relationship with Google
- Auto ads simplify placement for non-technical publishers
- Scales across entire site archive automatically

### AdSense cons

- Requires approval and ongoing policy compliance
- RPM varies wildly by geography and niche ($1–$30+ RPM range is common)
- Low traffic yields coffee money, not rent
- Ad layouts can hurt UX and Core Web Vitals if overloaded
- Account bans are difficult to appeal — policy mistakes are costly

### Typical AdSense fit

Productivity tips, general tech explainers, programming tutorials without a single tool recommendation, local guides, and broad SEO content where readers browse multiple pages per session.

## How affiliate marketing works

Affiliate programs pay you when a reader completes a defined action — usually a purchase, sometimes a free trial signup — through a tracked link. Amazon Associates, software SaaS programs (ConvertKit, hosting providers), and aggregate networks (ShareASale, Impact) are common entry points.

You choose products, write reviews or comparisons, insert disclosure-compliant links, and earn **commission percentages** (4% on Amazon to 30–50% on digital products).

### Affiliate pros

- Higher per-conversion payouts than ad clicks on commercial intent content
- No ad clutter — page stays clean
- Content aligns with reader goal ("best tool for X")
- Can work with low traffic if intent is high (buyer keywords)
- Multiple programs diversify income beyond one network

### Affiliate cons

- Links break when products change, prices update, or programs revoke accounts
- Requires disclosure and compliance (FTC, ASA guidelines)
- Income is volatile — seasonal spikes and droughts
- Some niches are saturated with affiliate SEO
- Amazon and others cut rates periodically

### Typical affiliate fit

"Best hosting for bloggers," software comparisons, gear reviews, course recommendations, and any query where the searcher is close to purchase.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Factor | AdSense | Affiliate |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| Traffic needed | Higher for meaningful income | Lower if intent is commercial |
| Setup effort | Moderate (approval, ad tuning) | High (research, updates, disclosures) |
| Content type | General informational | Product-focused, reviews, comparisons |
| Income stability | Steadier at scale | Spiky, commission-dependent |
| UX impact | Ads visible | Minimal if links are contextual |
| Policy risk | Google program policies | FTC disclosure, merchant TOS |
| Maintenance | Low ongoing | High — keep links and prices current |

Neither is passive income in the early months. Both require traffic and trust.

## Earnings realism for new sites

Assume a new tech/productivity blog six months in:

**AdSense scenario:** 5,000 monthly pageviews, $8 RPM → roughly $40/month. At 50,000 pageviews and $12 RPM → $600/month. Geography and seasonality move these numbers.

**Affiliate scenario:** Same 5,000 pageviews but on a "best email tool" post ranking page one. 200 clicks, 3% conversion, $50 average commission → $300/month from one strong page. Other pages earn zero.

Affiliate concentrates earnings in money pages; AdSense spreads micro-revenue across the catalog.

## Can you use both?

Yes — many publishers run AdSense site-wide and affiliate links inside relevant posts. Guidelines:

- Do not clutter comparison posts with ads above the fold — readers leave before seeing your affiliate recommendation
- Disclose affiliate relationships clearly; AdSense does not replace disclosure obligations
- Avoid affiliate links on pages that violate merchant rules (some SaaS programs restrict paid traffic or certain ad combinations)
- Test whether combined monetization hurts time-on-page; sometimes choosing one per page type wins

A sensible hybrid:

- **AdSense** on evergreen explainers and newsy posts
- **Affiliate** on buyer-intent comparisons and tool tutorials you genuinely use

## Choosing a primary strategy by niche

### Web development and technical tutorials

Default: **AdSense** on broad tutorials; **affiliate** on hosting, IDE, course, and book recommendations embedded naturally. Developers tolerate ads less than popups — keep units modest.

### Productivity and solo business

Mixed. Listicles ("best writing tools") lean affiliate; workflow essays lean AdSense.

### Pure journalism or opinion

**AdSense** — affiliate links feel incongruent unless reviewing products.

### Finance and YMYL topics

Both are scrutinized heavily. AdSense applies stricter policies; affiliate requires expertise and compliance. Build E-E-A-T before aggressive monetization.

## Getting started: AdSense path

1. Publish 15–25 quality posts (rules of thumb vary; quality wins)
2. Complete About, Contact, Privacy Policy
3. Apply at [google.com/adsense](https://www.google.com/adsense)
4. After approval, start with Auto ads or two manual units below content
5. Monitor RPM in AdSense and UX in Analytics — reduce ad load if bounce spikes

Rejection is feedback. Fix thin content, navigation, or policy gaps before reapplying.

## Getting started: affiliate path

1. Use products you already pay for — authenticity converts
2. Join official programs (not sketchy aggregators)
3. Add disclosure at top of monetized posts: "This post contains affiliate links"
4. Track links with UTM parameters or program dashboards
5. Update posts when products change — stale reviews erode trust and rankings

Start with one program aligned to your content (hosting if you write about deploying sites).

## Long-term strategy: stack as you grow

Year one: pick **one primary** model to optimize content around. Chasing both without traffic splits focus.

Year two with 30k+ monthly sessions: layer the second model, experiment with newsletter sponsorships, and sell digital products if audience trust is strong.

The highest-earning solo publishers diversify — but diversification follows audience scale, not the other way around.

## Decision framework

Answer three questions:

1. **What does my reader search for?** Information → AdSense lean. Products → affiliate lean.
2. **How much traffic do I realistically have in six months?** Under 10k monthly → affiliate on high-intent pages may beat AdSense everywhere.
3. **How much maintenance time do I want?** Low → AdSense. Willing to update reviews quarterly → affiliate.

There is no moral superiority to either model. Readers care that recommendations are honest and disclosures are clear.

## Final recommendation for new publishers

If you run a general publishing blog about web dev, productivity, and SEO — **start AdSense after meeting program requirements** while **adding affiliate links only where you make genuine tool recommendations**. Measure for ninety days. Double down on whichever earns more per hour of maintenance.

Monetization should not drive topic choice before you have an audience. Write useful posts first, understand what ranks, then align revenue with reader intent. AdSense and affiliate marketing are tools, not business models by themselves — the asset is still trust, and trust compounds slower than any ad script loads.
